## AR-01 — Linear/Modular Baseline

architecture · domain 02 · [AR-01 — Linear/Modular Baseline](../../architectures/linear-modular.md)

A sequential reference composition separates normalization, candidate generation, comparison evidence, scoring, decision policy, and entity construction through explicit artifacts. It supports controlled component replacement without choosing a mandatory matching algorithm or resolution strategy. Use it when traceable experiments and understandable interfaces matter. Identity semantics, candidate scope, and score interpretation must be specified first; simplicity does not remove candidate omissions, calibration problems, or downstream merge risks.

## AR-02 — Recall-Oriented Multi-Pass

architecture · domain 02 · [AR-02 — Recall-Oriented Multi-Pass](../../architectures/recall-multi-pass.md)

Multiple retrieval passes propose overlapping candidate pairs, which are unioned, deduplicated, and scored under a declared downstream policy. The pattern targets missed matches caused by heterogeneous names, identifiers, scripts, or sparse attributes without dictating a scorer. It requires complementary retrieval evidence and explicit eligibility rules. Candidate coverage can increase before pruning, but workload, selection bias, and false-positive exposure can also grow; pass value must be assessed marginally rather than by pass count.

Tags: `candidate-generation`, `entity-agnostic`, `high-recall`, `multi-pass`, `multilingual`, `multiscript`, `noisy`, `provenance`, `sparse`.

## AR-03 — Precision-First Adaptive Cascade

architecture · domain 02 · [AR-03 — Precision-First Adaptive Cascade](../../architectures/precision-cascade.md)

A cascade routes uncertain cases to progressively more costly evidence, scorers, or human review while allowing justified early decisions. It targets asymmetric false-merge costs and uneven case difficulty, not a universal ordering of models. Reliable early evidence, explicit routing units, and reversible dispositions are prerequisites. Compute savings depend on how often cases can safely exit; downstream distribution shift and irreversible early mistakes can erase the benefit, so route-specific quality and end-to-end costs must be tested together.

Tags: `asymmetric-costs`, `entity-agnostic`, `evaluation`, `high-precision`, `human-review`, `provenance`, `uncertainty`.

## AR-04 — Parallel Multi-Model/Ensemble

architecture · domain 02 · [AR-04 — Parallel Multi-Model/Ensemble](../../architectures/parallel-ensemble.md)

Several scorers examine shared or explicitly unioned candidates, then a fusion policy combines outputs or sends disagreements to review. The pattern seeks complementary evidence and error behavior rather than assuming more models are better. It requires compatible candidate keys, declared score meanings, and leakage-safe training when fusion is learned. Costs include multiple inference paths, calibration, and monitoring; correlated errors, incomparable scores, and silently missing branch outputs can undermine both accuracy and interpretability.

Tags: `calibration`, `compute-intensive`, `entity-agnostic`, `evaluation`, `human-review`, `pairwise`, `uncertainty`.

## AR-05 — Collective/Consistency-Aware Resolution

architecture · domain 02 · [AR-05 — Collective/Consistency-Aware Resolution](../../architectures/collective-consistency.md)

Resolution decisions are coupled when identity semantics impose cardinality, membership constraints, or useful relational dependencies. The pattern covers one-to-one assignment with unmatched outcomes, constrained clustering, and genuinely collective relational inference without treating them as equivalent. It requires justified constraints and score semantics compatible with the resolution objective. Joint decisions may prevent locally plausible conflicts, but incorrect constraints or propagated evidence errors can harm whole groups; a graph representation and iterative inference are not mandatory.

## AR-06 — Incremental/Continuous Entity Resolution

architecture · domain 02 · [AR-06 — Incremental/Continuous Entity Resolution](../../architectures/incremental-continuous.md)

Versioned changes to observations, models, or policies trigger recomputation of affected candidates, evidence, decisions, and entity views instead of routinely restarting every stage. The lifecycle pattern can wrap linear, multi-pass, cascaded, ensemble, or consistency-aware matching. It requires explicit event semantics, dependency tracking, stable identity handling, and reversible updates. Locality may lower update cost, but deletions, long-range dependencies, drift, and accumulated historical mistakes can force wider repair or replay; full recomputation is a comparator, not ground truth.

Tags: `constraints`, `entity-agnostic`, `evaluation`, `governance`, `incremental`, `monitoring`, `provenance`, `streaming`.

decision · domain 02 · [DEC-02-001 — Choose pipeline boundaries and extension mechanisms from expected change](../../decisions/pipeline-modularity.md)

## SOL-02-001 — Replaceable-stage pipelines with explicit semantic contracts

solution · domain 02 · [SOL-02-001 — Replaceable-stage pipelines with explicit semantic contracts](../../solutions/architecture/replaceable-stage-pipelines.md)

Separate pipeline responsibilities through explicit artifacts and semantic compatibility checks, starting from ordinary functions rather than a mandatory plugin framework. Introduce hooks, strategies, validated configuration, policies, or dependency scheduling only at justified change points. Preserve source, population, time, score, and state meaning across substitutions; expose global dependencies and migration obligations. Controlled refactor and extension experiments assess behavior and engineering costs. Equivalent outputs do not establish identity truth, and modularity is not an observed accuracy gain.
