# Codex Task: Entity Resolution Atlas v1.2+ — Comprehensive Knowledge & Agent-Utility Expansion

## 0. Mission

Extend the existing Entity Resolution Solution Atlas into a substantially more useful, technically rigorous, agent-friendly external knowledge base.

This is an implementation task, not merely a planning exercise.

You are authorized to implement a substantial expansion across multiple iterations. Do not artificially constrain the scope to a handful of cards if additional coverage is justified.

However, do not mass-generate shallow documents, fill every taxonomy gap, or introduce complexity solely for completeness.

**The objective is quality, coverage of important decisions, and practical utility for AI-assisted development and research.**

The Atlas should help an AI agent:

1. Understand unfamiliar Entity Resolution problems.
2. Identify relevant architectural and algorithmic choices.
3. Compare credible alternatives using sound theoretical reasoning.
4. Design modular, maintainable, reproducible ER pipelines.
5. Select ML, statistical, NLP, rule-based and graph approaches appropriately.
6. Work effectively when reliable ground truth is scarce or absent.
7. Plan controlled experiments and interpret evidence correctly.
8. Identify failure modes and cross-module interactions.
9. Produce actionable, testable implementation plans for an external project.
10. Avoid repeatedly researching the same concepts and rediscovering already-known trade-offs.

The Atlas must remain **project-agnostic**.

The supplied project audits are useful examples of the problems an ER system can encounter. They are not requirements to reproduce the audited project or document its implementation.

# 1. Non-negotiable design principles

## 1.1 Preserve the Atlas architecture

Start by inspecting the actual repository.

Relevant contracts include:

- `AGENTS.md`
- `SCOPE.md`
- `SCHEMA.md`
- `README.md`
- `CATALOG.md`
- `taxonomy/`
- `taxonomy/module-contracts.md`
- `templates/`
- `guides/`
- `references/evidence-policy.md`
- `references/atlas-manifest.json`
- `validation/agent-utility/`

Preserve the existing five knowledge-object types:

- Decision
- Solution
- Architecture
- Hypothesis
- Evaluation

Preserve the 14-domain taxonomy, existing stable IDs and the six AR-01–AR-06 architectures.

Do not invent another taxonomy, duplicate knowledge-object system or a parallel navigation framework.

Architectural patterns can be expressed through Decision/Solution Cards, guides, module contracts and deep dives without introducing new architecture IDs.

Existing v1.1 stop/review restrictions are superseded by this explicit authorization for a substantial expansion. Other integrity, provenance and evaluation restrictions remain applicable.

## 1.2 Universal knowledge, not project documentation

Do not introduce specific project tables, source names, functions, directories, thresholds or configuration settings as universal Atlas concepts.

Generalize observed problems into reusable engineering and scientific questions.

For example:

- Hardcoded SQL guards → rule composition and policy-engine design.
- Source-specific probability weights → source-aware evidence reliability.
- Large connected components → graph contamination diagnostics.
- No labeled matches → incomplete-reference evaluation strategies.
- Expensive pair generation → retrieval cost/recall trade-offs.

A method's behavior may be illustrated with synthetic examples involving persons, organizations or financial entities, but without embedding private project details.

## 1.3 Depth over superficial coverage

Every substantive card must explain:

- What problem it addresses.
- The mechanism and theoretical reasoning.
- Why it could improve an outcome.
- Under which conditions it helps.
- Under which conditions it fails or hurts.
- Dependencies and assumptions.
- Alternatives, including a credible simpler baseline.
- Computational and operational implications.
- Interaction with other ER components.
- How to evaluate it.
- What evidence would justify adopting or rejecting it.

Avoid statements such as "method X improves precision" without explaining the mechanism and conditions.

Use worked examples and equations where they materially clarify the decision.

An informative 1,200-word card is preferable to an artificial 700-word card missing important theory. Do not inflate documents merely to reach a target length.

## 1.4 Evidence discipline

Distinguish:

- Established mathematical implications.
- Findings supported by external literature.
- Empirical results established only in specific contexts.
- Plausible but untested hypotheses.
- Open questions or insufficient evidence.

Use the existing source ledger and citation conventions.

Verify primary literature and author-maintained technical documentation where possible.

Never fabricate references, benchmark results or expected improvements.

If external verification is unavailable, record the limitation and avoid presenting an unsupported claim as established evidence.

## 1.5 Agent-friendly, selective retrieval

Preserve:

L0 discovery → L1 comparison → L2 detailed knowledge → L3 specialized depth.

An agent should be able to find relevant knowledge without reading the entire repository.

As the corpus expands, strengthen routing and navigation instead of increasing the default reading volume.

# 3. Phase 1 — Evaluation and research without reliable ground truth

This is the highest-priority scientific expansion.

Existing `SOL-10-001`, `EVAL-11-001...004`, and the calibration deep dive form the foundation. Extend them rather than repeating their existing theory.

## 3.1 Ground-truth acquisition

Develop a connected decision/solution family comparing:

- Independent probability sampling.
- Stratified sampling.
- Entity-centric adjudication.
- Uncertainty-based active learning.
- Disagreement sampling.
- Cluster-risk sampling.
- High-confidence silver labels.
- Weakly supervised labels.
- Human review and adjudication.
- Hybrid designs combining independent evaluation samples and targeted training/research samples.

Explain annotation units, sampling populations, inclusion probabilities, class imbalance, review costs, disagreement handling and selection bias.

A particularly important distinction:

**A sample optimized to discover informative cases is not automatically a representative evaluation sample.**

Provide practical designs for gradually building reliable reference evidence while preserving independent evaluation.

## 3.2 Evaluation under incomplete reference coverage

Extend the theoretical and practical treatment of:

- Pair-level precision and recall.
- Entity-level and cluster-level quality.
- Overmerge and undermerge errors.
- False negatives outside candidate generation.
- Missing labels versus negative labels.
- Positive-unlabeled evaluation.
- Partial entity truth.
- Sampling-weighted estimates.
- Dependence-aware uncertainty intervals.
- Rare but high-cost failure modes.
- Tail-risk diagnostics.

Define estimands, populations and denominators explicitly.

Explain what is identifiable from the available evidence and what is not.

Distinguish quality diagnostics from actual accuracy estimation.

For instance, graph contradictions can indicate suspicious clusters but cannot independently certify population-level precision.

## 3.3 Synthetic, perturbation and metamorphic testing

Develop dedicated reusable guidance for:

- Synthetic ER fixtures.
- Controlled duplicates.
- Controlled identity-changing transformations.
- Invariant-preserving transformations.
- Metamorphic testing.
- Counterexample-driven tests.
- Property-based testing.
- Regression suites for matching and resolution policies.

Illustrative cases:

- Formatting changes should not cause arbitrary identity changes.
- Adding irrelevant metadata should not unexpectedly alter a decision.
- A source's record-processing order should not alter results unless an explicitly documented stateful policy permits it.
- Reusing an identifier from a different namespace must not establish identity.
- A company subsidiary must not be merged with its parent merely because names overlap.
- A weak bridge edge must not automatically be interpreted as reliable cluster-wide identity evidence.

Explain why synthetic success does not establish real-world model accuracy.

## 3.4 Weak supervision and active learning

Cover:

- Labeling functions.
- Rule agreement and disagreement.
- Correlated labeling functions.
- Label-model assumptions.
- Self-training and pseudo-labeling.
- Active learning acquisition functions.
- Source-specific label scarcity.
- Confidence and abstention.
- Feedback loops.
- Circular supervision.

Explicitly address the risk of training a model on labels derived from the same rules later used to evaluate that model.

Separate:

- Rule imitation.
- Improved discrimination.
- Independent quality improvement.

## 3.5 Experiment design

Add a dedicated experimentation decision family covering:

- Baseline-controlled experiments.
- Stage-specific ablations.
- Fixed candidate-set comparisons.
- Factorial experiments.
- Error slicing.
- Independent holdouts.
- Entity-disjoint and temporal splits.
- Data leakage.
- Statistical uncertainty.
- Practical significance versus statistical significance.
- Repeated runs and stability.
- Negative and inconclusive results.

Every experimental design must explain which competing mechanisms it can distinguish.

Deliverables should include reusable experiment-planning templates and evaluation routes.

## 5.1 Source-aware probabilistic scoring

Deepen existing Fellegi–Sunter, additive and logistic-scoring content.

- Manual m/u parameter specification.
- EM assumptions and identifiability.
- Conditional dependence.
- Class priors.
- Candidate-selection effects.

- Sparse and missing fields.
- Source-specific evidence reliability.
- Global versus source-specific models.

- Shift and transfer diagnostics.

When does pooling evidence across sources improve statistical efficiency, and when does it hide important heterogeneity?

## 5.2 Advanced supervised and semi-supervised scoring

Cover the important practical alternatives:

- Regularized logistic models.
- Constrained or interpretable GAMs.
- Gradient-boosted trees.

- Hybrid rules plus learned scores.
- Embedding-based matching.
- Selective use of neural/LLM comparators.

## 5.3 NLP and entity-name comparison

- Multi-representation names.

# 9. Implementation workflow across multiple sessions

This task is intentionally larger than one short coding-agent run.

Implement it in coherent waves:

**Wave 0 — Baseline and implementation tracker**

**Wave 1 — Unlabeled evaluation, research design, annotation and weak supervision**

**Wave 2 — Architecture, modularity, rule engines and experiment-friendly MLOps**

**Wave 3 — NLP, blocking, statistical scoring, source-aware ML and graph methods**

**Wave 4 — Scalability, monitoring, incremental identity and governance**

**Wave 5 — Agent retrieval, integration, benchmark and final quality review**

These are implementation waves, not scope limits.

Within each wave:

1. Review existing relevant objects.
2. Identify genuine knowledge gaps.
3. Implement or deepen related objects.
4. Add meaningful cross-links.
5. Update navigation and manifests.
6. Validate structure.
7. Review scientific substance.
8. Record completion and unresolved issues.

Do not stop after writing an expansion plan.

Do not ask for review between waves unless a genuine ambiguity or external dependency prevents responsible implementation.

If execution budget or context becomes limited, finish the current coherent wave and create a detailed handoff containing:

- Completed work.
- Files changed.
- Validation results.
- Remaining work.
- Important design decisions.
- Known blockers.
- Exact recommended next action.

Do not silently drop an unfinished wave.

# 10. Final acceptance criteria

The expansion is complete when the following conditions hold:

### Knowledge coverage

- Important gaps discussed in this task are implemented or explicitly deferred with technical justification.
- Evaluation without labels has substantial operational and theoretical coverage.
- Agent-friendly architecture and modularity choices are covered.
- Hybrid rule-based/ML architecture is addressed.
- Relevant ML/statistical/NLP/retrieval/graph alternatives are meaningfully compared.
- MLOps, lifecycle and scalability knowledge is integrated.

### Knowledge quality

- New material contains meaningful mechanisms, assumptions and counterexamples.
- Alternatives are compared against baselines.
- Claims are evidence-aware.
- Evaluation methods match their estimands.
- No invented empirical improvements or universal guarantees.
- No unnecessary duplicate content.

### Agent utility

- Relevant knowledge can be retrieved selectively.
- Important decisions link to alternatives and evaluations.
- The Atlas can produce actionable experiment and development plans.
- Cross-module interactions are discoverable.
- Project-specific details remain outside the canonical Atlas.
- Benchmark results and their limitations are reported accurately.

### Engineering integrity

- Existing Atlas contracts remain coherent.
- Stable IDs and historical artifacts are preserved.
- Generated indexes are current.
- Structural tests pass, or any failures are explicitly documented.
- The expansion is organized into coherent, maintainable knowledge families.
